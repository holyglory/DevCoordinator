# DC-2026-07-18-INVITES-01 — Protected access is exact and owner-controlled

## Durable decision

The Console retains a verified Google identity even when it has no grant, but
denies every protected HTTP and WebSocket resource until a configured owner
approves that exact Console or routed-domain request. Invited accounts and
resource grants live in private atomic server policy and are rechecked on every
request. Configured owners remain the recovery and administration boundary.

After Google identity and the exact route grant pass, the edge strips
caller-supplied authorization and may inject a private route-scoped Bearer or
Basic credential. The credential never appears in ordinary browser-visible
route state, and only configured owners may change it. Public routes preserve
their own end-to-end authentication. The trusted TLS edge and automated DNS
certificate renewal remain explicit production dependencies; all operational
mutations still go through DevCoordinator.

Missing browser identity and a rejected server-held upstream credential are
different failures. Browser navigation without identity returns to the exact
requested page after sign-in; rejection after route authorization becomes a
branded configuration failure without forwarding the upstream body or
authentication challenge.

An explicitly configured protected development route that needs a human audit
actor may receive a maximum-15-second Ed25519 assertion only after Google
identity and exact route authorization pass. It is bound to issuer, public
host, route grant, HTTP method, normalized email, and one-time identifier; the
edge strips caller assertions, publishes only the public key, and the upstream
must validate signature, claims, and replay before mapping permissions.

Each Console-authorized account may register and manage only its own Telegram
bots unless it is a configured owner. Bot tokens remain private server state,
subscriptions bind to immutable Coordinator repository IDs, and every private
chat user requires separate approval after `/start`. Notifications consume
durable Coordinator events through a restart-safe cursor and outbox, so starts,
stops, failures, and crashes are not limited to Console-originated actions.

## Alternatives rejected

A global allowlist cannot express per-resource access. Automatic approval or a
client-supplied identity bypasses the owner boundary. Passing upstream Basic
authentication through creates a second browser login and exposes the wrong
credential boundary. A plain trusted header is forgeable by another loopback
caller, while a fixed private credential identifies only the Console service,
not the human. Shared bots, display-name subscriptions, Console action hooks,
and best-effort sends mix ownership or lose events during outages.

## Verification contract

Tests must cover denied-but-identified requests, exact owner approval, grant
revocation, HTTP and WebSocket checks, caller authorization stripping, private
credential injection and rejection, signed-assertion claim/replay validation,
bot ownership, per-user approval, immutable project binding, cursor replay, and
secret redaction. Live activation remains in the ledger until the public
Console and an approved private Telegram chat prove the deployed journey.
